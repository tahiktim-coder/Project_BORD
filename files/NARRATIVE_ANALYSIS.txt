================================================================================
  3. THE PLAYER'S PERSONAL ARC — COMPLICITY PACING
================================================================================

  Current pacing of "you are complicit" reveals:

    Case 4:  "Your processing stamp is on every page." (25% through game)
    Case 8:  "Clerk ID matches your terminal." (44%)
    Case 11: "Including you." (61%)
    Case 14: "This is your file. 214 intake forms." (78%)

  THE PROBLEM:

    Case 4 is a BOMBSHELL delivered to a player who doesn't know:
      - That they have a processing stamp
      - That they've worked here before tonight
      - That they have any history with Facility 6
      - Who they even ARE beyond "Case Adjudicator, Level 3"

    The intro says: "You are Case Adjudicator, Level 3."
    It does NOT say: "You have been here for [X] years."
    It does NOT say: "You have processed [X] cases before tonight."
    It does NOT say anything about the player's life, history, or stakes.

    When Case 4 says "your stamp is on every page," the player thinks:
    "What stamp? I just started!" — confusion, not horror.

  THE FIX — SEED THE INTRO WITH PERSONAL STAKES:

    Current intro:
      "You are Case Adjudicator, Level 3."
      "Facility 6 was demolished last month. That is not relevant to
       your duties."
      "Process the cases. Consult the Bureau Manual. Follow the directive."
      "Begin."

    Proposed intro (5 lines instead of 4):
      ---------------------------------------------------------------
      "Night Processing. Your third year."
      "You are Case Adjudicator, Level 3. Your stamp is on file."
      "Facility 6 was demolished last month. That is not relevant to
       your duties."
      "Process the cases. Consult the Bureau Manual. Follow the directive."
      "Begin."
      ---------------------------------------------------------------

    WHY THIS WORKS:
    - "Third year" — you've been here. This is not your first night.
    - "Your stamp is on file" — plants the word STAMP. When Case 4 says
      "your processing stamp is on every page," the player recalls the
      intro. The stamp was mentioned. It was always there.
    - Everything else stays the same. Two new phrases. Maximum impact.

  ALTERNATIVE INTRO (more atmospheric, "finally you found the job" feel):
      ---------------------------------------------------------------
      "Night Processing. Third year. Same desk."
      "You requested this shift. The pay is better and nobody asks
       questions."
      "Facility 6 was demolished last month. That is not relevant to
       your duties."
      "Process the cases. Consult the Bureau Manual. Follow the directive."
      "Begin."
      ---------------------------------------------------------------

    WHY THIS WORKS:
    - "Same desk" — continuity. This is a life, not a tutorial.
    - "You requested this shift" — the player CHOSE to be here.
      Complicity starts at the intro, not at Case 4.
    - "Nobody asks questions" — the player already knows the deal.
      They chose ignorance. This makes Case 4 feel like the past
      catching up, not a surprise.

  ADDITIONAL SETUP — CASE 1 DETAIL TWEAK:

    Current Case 1 detail:
      "He sold papers outside Re-Education Facility 6 for eleven years.
       He saw the trucks."

    Add one sentence to Case 1 (the FIRST thing the player reads):
      ---------------------------------------------------------------
      "He sold papers outside Re-Education Facility 6 for eleven years.
       He saw the trucks. His intake form is in your filing cabinet."
      ---------------------------------------------------------------

    WHY: This plants the idea that the player has a FILING CABINET full
    of prior work. It normalizes the stamp reveal in Case 4. It also
    means Selby himself was processed by you before — you're now
    deciding his fate for a second time. Devastating if the player
    catches it.

    WARNING: This might be too much too early. Test both versions.
    The intro change is essential. The Case 1 addition is optional.

    The player sits down and is told "You are Case Adjudicator, Level 3."

    Papers Please opens with: "Your family needs you." Your apartment

    personal stakes beyond the person on the paper.

    BORD doesn't need family mechanics. But it needs ONE line that
    makes the player feel like a person who CHOSE to sit here.

  WITH PROPOSED INTRO (EDIT 1):
  =============================
    0:00  Title screen. Begin Shift.
    0:05  "Night Processing. Third year. Same desk."
          "You are Case Adjudicator, Level 3. Your stamp is on file."
          "Facility 6 was demolished last month..."

          "Begin."

    0:35  Camera down. Case 1.

    - "Third year" — the player has been here. They have a past.
    - "Same desk" — routine. Weariness. This is just another night.
    - "Your stamp is on file" — planted. Waiting. Will detonate in Case 4.

    Minute 1: "This is routine. I'm a bureaucrat. Process files."

================================================================================
  6. STRUCTURAL OVERVIEW — THE THREE SHIFTS AS THREE ACTS
================================================================================

    SHIFT 1: WHAT HAPPENED?
    -----------------------
    Cases are about OTHER people. The player is a judge.
    Each case adds a piece of the Facility 6 puzzle.
    Emotional distance: HIGH. You're processing strangers.
    Ends with: You know what F6 was (organ harvesting).

    SHIFT 2: WHO DID IT?
    --------------------
    Cases start pointing at the SYSTEM — and at YOU.
    Henrik Voss (Case 8) = you're being watched.
    Amara Osei (Case 9) = someone like you made F6 work.
    14 Witnesses (Case 11) = they know what you did.
    Emotional distance: SHRINKING. The desk is a mirror.
    Ends with: You know the witnesses exist. You chose their fate.

    SHIFT 3: WHAT WILL YOU DO?
    --------------------------
    Cases are about CONSEQUENCES and POWER.
    Nadia Kader (12) = your past choices echo forward.
    Dr. Rask (13) = the number 214. Your number.
    Your file (14) = the mirror shatters.
