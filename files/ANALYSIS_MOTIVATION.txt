BORD ANALYSIS: PLAYER MOTIVATION & "WHY TONIGHT?"
===================================================

CORE PROBLEM: If the player has been compliant for years, why change tonight?

ANSWER: THE CASES CHANGED, NOT YOU.

You've been here for years stamping routine paperwork - tax forms, residency filings,
budget requisitions. You never saw what was behind the paper. F6 was just a name on
a form. Tonight, for the first time, the F6 closure cases landed on your desk.

WHY THIS WORKS:
- "Why tonight?" answered: these cases are new
- Stamp in Case 4 works: you DID stamp F6 intake forms, as routine paperwork
- Case 14 (214 forms) works: you stamped children into a facility without knowing
- Case 8 (Henrik Voss) works: Bureau catches you hesitating on F6 cases specifically
- Aligns with Hannah Arendt - bureaucracies compartmentalize

PROPOSED INTRO:
  "Night Processing. Same desk."
  "Facility 6 was demolished last month. The cases from that closure arrived tonight."
  "You are Case Adjudicator, Level 3. Process them like any other."
  "Begin."

WHY EACH LINE:
- Line 1: universe + you (routine, weariness)
- Line 2: WHY TONIGHT - the critical missing piece
- Line 3: who you are + instruction (and a lie - these are NOT like any other)
- Line 4: start

WHY OTHER FRAMINGS FAIL:
- First night: destroys stamp/complicity arc (Cases 4, 11, 14 all break)
- Already merciful: Henrik Voss says "current batch" - deviations are from tonight
- No backstory: game already commits to a past through Cases 4, 11, 14
- "Third year": creates the plot hole the user identified

THE PLAYER ARC UNDER THIS FRAMING:
- Shift 1: "I stamped WHAT?" - seeing F6 people for the first time
- Shift 2: "They see me seeing" - Bureau notices your hesitation
- Shift 3: "My stamp, my hands, my choice" - the mirror shatters

The player character is not suddenly becoming good.
They are suddenly becoming informed.
